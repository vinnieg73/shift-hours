How it works
  • 12 PM is noon and 12 AM is midnight.
  • A dot or comma means decimal hours: 9.5 is 9:30 and 9.25 is 9:15. For clock minutes type 930 or 9:30.
  • If the end time is earlier than the start, the shift runs past midnight into the next day.
  • 15-minute rounding uses the 7-minute rule: 9:07 rounds down to 9:00, 9:08 rounds up to 9:15.
  • 5-minute rounding: :01–:02 round down, :03–:04 round up. 6-minute (tenth of an hour): :01–:02 down, :03–:05 up.
  • Decimal hours = minutes ÷ 60. For example 7:15 is 7.25 hours.
  • Typing tips:
  • The weekly timesheet uses the same clock format and rounding as above. Break is in minutes.

    How per diem works
    • Per diem is a daily allowance for hotel (lodging) plus meals and incidentals (M&IE) when you travel for work.
    • Lodging is a cap per night, and it can change by month. You're paid what the room cost, up to the cap. Taxes are claimed separately.
    • M&IE is the full daily rate on middle days and 75% on the day you leave and the day you return.
    • If a meal was given to you (at a conference, say), its amount comes off that day. Meals on a plane or a free hotel breakfast don't count.
    • GSA sets rates by county. A ZIP gives GSA's exact answer; a city that crosses into another rate area lets you pick.
    • Places not on GSA's list get the standard rate. GSA's year runs Oct 1 to Sep 30.
    • Covers the 48 states and DC. Alaska, Hawaii and U.S. territories use Defense Department rates.
    • Rates: GSA per diem files, FY 2027. Place names: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) and the U.S. Census Bureau. Check with your employer; their policy may differ.
