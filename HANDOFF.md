# HANDOFF: Shift Hours

Last updated: 2026-11-19 (Defense Department rates rebuilt from the November 2026 file: Kahului and Kona get their own areas; earlier the same day the sweep was merged to `main`)

## What it is

A single-file shift hours calculator (`index.html`, no build step) served by GitHub Pages at shifthours.app (`CNAME`). State lives in the visitor's `localStorage` under `shift-hours-v1`.

It has three views, switched with the Hours | Per diem | Months control under the header: **Hours** (the calculator, punch clock, weekly timecard and backup), **Per diem** (GSA and Defense Department lodging and M&IE rates for any U.S. city; its rate data is `data/perdiem-fy2027.json`, fetched only when that view opens) and **Months** (working hours in each month, computed in the page).

Files: `index.html` (everything), `sw.js` (offline cache), `qr.js` (vendored QR library, loaded only by Move), `manifest.webmanifest` and the icon PNGs, `data/` (rate file), `scripts/` (yearly rate build and checks), `tests/` (Playwright page tests). `README.md` is the short public description; `CHANGELOG.md` is the history of every change.

## Status

## How it works now

- **Look: Graphite Tool.** One solid panel per view, 1px hairlines, 6px control and 8px panel corners, Inter labels, Chivo Mono for every number and micro-label (plain zero), keycaps with a 1px solid lip, sliding thumbs on segmented controls. No blur, no translucency, no soft shadows. Light (warm off-white) and dark (warm graphite) follow the device (`prefers-color-scheme`); there is no in-page switch. Two colours carry meaning: the carbon ink `--stamp` / `--stamp-wash` (frames, focus rings, selected keys) and the ember `--ember` (answers and whatever is live or current). Red (`--alert`) is only for errors and "not counted".
- **Layout.** Phones (640px and under): Start/End card, result, then Breaks and Settings; header on one row. 360px and under: tighter keys and week rows. Desktop (900px and up): two columns capped at 960px, entry and options left, result right, the week full width below; Settings opens by itself only here. `<form id="form">` is the `.grid`; on phones `.col-in` is `display: contents` and the cards are placed with `order`.
- **Hours.** Start and End take `845`, `8:45`, or `8.75` (a dot or comma is decimal hours, and every decimal read shows a "Read 9.30 as 9:18" badge). 12 or 24-hour; `24:00` only in 24-hour mode. An end earlier than the start runs past midnight. Up to three breaks, as minutes or as start and end (clipped to the shift, overlaps merged). Rounding 15 (7-minute rule), 6 or 5 minutes, optionally for breaks too. The answer is decimal hours in the readout with h:mm under it, each with Copy. Totals never include a guess: a bad or half-filled break pauses the total with a badge that says why. A first visit is blank everywhere (faint example placeholders, a faint 0.00), breaks included.
- **Punch clock.** Punch in stamps the start and keeps the end on the clock (`S.live`, `S.liveAt`; re-stamped every 30 s by `tick()`, and when the page comes back). Typing the end lets go of the clock; Punch out stamps `S.outAt`. More than 24 h live stops the clock and empties the end box. A start retyped after punching out is a correction within 3 hours of the punch, and a new shift beyond that. A punched shift counts the real time across a clock change (`clockChange()`; typed shifts stay wall-clock).
- **Add to day.** Copies start, end and the deducted break minutes into a day's row: the day punched in (within 36 h), else today. The key names that day and is kept current by `addKey()` on every render and every tick. A filled day asks "Replace Fri?" (two taps). It files the punch (`liveAt` and `outAt` cleared) and stores a clock-change correction in the row as `week[i].adj`.
- **Weekly timecard.** Mon to Sun; a finished day folds to one line, an unfinished one stays open with a red note, empty days are dashed add keys. The total is the sum of minutes; an unfinished or invalid day is left out, named in a red line, and blocks Copy. Clear week takes two taps.
- **Saving and tabs.** State is in `localStorage` (`shift-hours-v1`), written 250 ms after an edit and on `pagehide`, only when it differs from what this tab last read or wrote. Another tab's save is taken (`adopt()`) unless this tab has an edit waiting to be written. `normalise()` rebuilds state from a fixed shape. A side key, `shift-hours-v1x`, holds `outAt` and `adj` for tabs still running a page from before 2026-10-23 (see Open decisions).
- **Your data.** Save backup (a JSON file; the share sheet on touch devices, a download on computers), Restore (asks first, and says what it replaces and what stays), Move (a link and QR code, `#move=z…`, deflate plus base64url; `qr.js` loads on demand). A failed save is said out loud in this card.
- **Per diem.** City, state or ZIP gives the rate area, lodging by month, M&IE and its breakdown; a place in more than one area lets you pick. GSA rates for the 48 states and DC, Defense Department rates for Alaska, Hawaii and the territories (seasons can change mid-month; the trip total prices day by day). Optional trip total: 75% travel days, meals given taken off but never below the day's incidentals. State in `shift-hours-perdiem-v1`. Data is `data/perdiem-fy2027.json`, fetched when the tab first opens.
- **Months.** Working hours per month at 8 per weekday; the 11 federal holidays (5 U.S.C. 6103, observed dates) are marked and still count 8. All computed in the page; nothing saved but the remembered view.
- **Views.** Hours | Per diem | Months, remembered in `shift-hours-view`; `#hours`, `#perdiem` and `#months` in the address pick one.
- **Offline.** `sw.js` saves the page, manifest, icons, `qr.js` and fonts. The page and the rate file are network-first (4 s and 8 s, then the saved copy), so a deploy shows on the next online visit with no cache bump. Bump `V` only when `SHELL` changes.
- **Visitor counter.** Cloudflare Web Analytics beacon at the end of `index.html` (cookieless, manual snippet because DNS is not proxied).

## Per diem data

- **Sources** (all public, no keys):
  - GSA per diem files page, gsa.gov/travel/plan-a-trip/per-diem-rates/per-diem-files: the FY rates XLSX (`FY2027_PerDiemRates_Validated090126.xlsx`) and the "ZIP code file for developers" (`FY2027PerDiemZipCode_Validated090126.xlsx`).
  - M&IE tiers from gsa.gov's "M&IE breakdowns" page: $68 = 16/19/28/5, $74 = 18/20/31/5, $80 = 20/22/33/5, $86 = 22/23/36/5, $92 = 23/26/38/5 (in `MIE` in `scripts/build_perdiem.py`). The build fails if GSA uses a tier not in that table.
  - GeoNames US postal codes, download.geonames.org/export/zip/US.zip (CC BY 4.0, credited in "How per diem works").
  - Census 2020: www2.census.gov/geo/docs/reference/codes2020/ `national_county2020.txt`, `national_place_by_county2020.txt`, `national_cousub2020.txt`; and www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/zcta520/ `tab20_zcta520_county20_natl.txt`, `tab20_zcta520_place20_natl.txt`, `tab20_zcta520_cousub20_natl.txt`.
- **How a lookup works:** ZIP → GSA's ZIP file. Town (Census place, town or township) → the ZIP areas that overlap it, weighted by land; each part goes by its county (GSA sets rates by county), except "City limits of …" areas (Sedona, Santa Monica, Falmouth, Roanoke) and Los Angeles (Edwards AFB), where GSA's ZIP file decides. Areas under 5% of a town's land are dropped as slivers. Named cities in a city-limits area (Cambridge MA, Grapevine TX…) get that area outright. Counties are searchable too ("Queens County, NY"). Post office names that aren't Census places go by their ZIPs. Same-name towns in one state (e.g. "Washington township" in many Indiana counties) are one entry; if they fall in different rate areas, each choice is labelled with its county.
- **Data format** (`data/perdiem-fy2027.json`, 1.2 MB, about 430 KB gzipped): `areas` = [name, state, GSA's county text, M&IE, lodging Oct..Sep], index 0 = standard. `places` = per state, `|`-separated records `Name[~rank][=area | =area:label;area:label]` (no `=` means standard; outside CONUS means Defense Dept.). `zips` = one string of 11-character records: ZIP + place index (4, base 36) + area (2, base 36; `zz` = split ZIP, see `zx`; `yy` = outside CONUS). `zinf` = ZIPs not in GSA's file whose rate comes from same-name or nearest ZIPs.
- **Checks** (2026-10-16): `scripts/check_perdiem.py` runs the page's own lookup code (the `pd-core` block in index.html) over everything: 40,426/40,426 GSA ZIPs match GSA's file exactly (1,839 are split and offer a choice); 41,488/41,488 GeoNames ZIPs resolve (921 outside CONUS, 168 not in GSA's file); 29,547/29,547 GeoNames places and 43,791/43,791 Census places, towns and counties come up as the first suggestion; 829 places offer a choice (271 straddle a county line, 546 are same-name towns, 12 are post office names). `scripts/spotcheck_perdiem.mjs` compares 27 cities against GSA's API (2026-10-16: all 27 pass, plus all five M&IE tiers' breakfast/lunch/dinner/incidentals and 75% first/last day. 10 cases came from the API itself (5 NYC boroughs, both Kansas Citys, both Arlingtons, Sedona). DEMO_KEY then locked out for hours, so the other 17 (incl. Santa Monica, Falmouth/Hyannis, Grapevine's split ZIP, seasonal Gulf Shores and Jackson WY, Monowi = standard) were read off gsa.gov's own lookup page and run through the same pass rules).

## Alaska, Hawaii and the territories (Defense Department rates, 2026-10-17)

- GSA covers only the 48 states and DC. The Defense Travel Management Office (DTMO) sets Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands, by town or island, with seasons that need not fall on month edges. `scripts/build_oconus.py` folds those into the same data file after `build_perdiem.py` has run: one area per DoD location (6th element `{dod:1, seasons?:[['MM/DD','MM/DD',lodging,mie]…], mie?:[12]}`; the lodging and M&IE arrays hold the rate in force on the 1st of each month; "[OTHER]" becomes "Rest of <state>"), every GeoNames post office and ZIP in those states pointed at an area (by name, else Hawaii county -> island, else county, else the state's rest; a DoD location with no post office of its name, like Guam or Oahu, becomes a searchable place), and M&IE breakdowns for every OCONUS total added to `mie` (FTR Appendix B up to $265, above that 15% / 25% / 40% breakfast / lunch / dinner, rest incidentals). `data.oconus` records the source; the page (`pd-core` `DOD` set, `outside()`, `rateOn()`) treats those states as covered only when that key is present, so the old data file still works.
- Page behaviour: "Defense Department rate area: Anchorage, AK" line and badge; M&IE that changes with the season shows the month's figure plus a range badge; a season that starts mid-month gets a badge, and the trip total uses `rateOn()` day by day (rows say "by season").
- Sources, fetched on the Mac because the cloud cannot reach travel.dod.mil (it also wants a browser User-Agent): the OCONUS ASCII zip from `https://www.travel.dod.mil/Portals/119/Documents/Allowances/Per_Diem/OCONUS/ASCII/OCONUS-ASCII-<year>.zip` (monthly files `MM-DD-YYoconus.txt`, `;`-separated: country;location;season start;season end;lodging;local meals;proportional meals;incidentals;footnote no;footnote rate;max per diem;effective date; M&IE = local meals + incidentals), the State Department FTR Appendix B M&IE breakdown table (saved as `mie-breakdown.csv`: mie,breakfast,lunch,dinner,incidentals), GeoNames `PR/GU/VI/AS/MP.zip` and the AK/HI rows of `US.txt` (`geonames-AK-HI.txt`). They are kept on branch `oconus-src` in `src-dod/` (with SOURCES.md), never on main.
- Test: `tests/oconus.test.js` (pass a data file path to route it into the page, or "" for the repo's; `tests/run.sh` passes ""). DoD rates change more often than GSA's (DTMO posts monthly): rebuild from the newest `*oconus.txt` when a month's file changes the non-foreign rows. A DoD row named for one town wins over the island the town is on: the November 2026 file added KAHULUI ($242 lodging, $163 M&IE; the rest of Maui is $354 and $153) and KONA ($242 and $163; the rest of the island outside Hilo is $242 and $180). Both rows carry the "rest of Hawaii" rate, which looks odd but is what the official file says. `EXTRA_ALIAS` points the post office name Kailua Kona (ZIPs 96740, 96745) at KONA.

## Yearly per diem refresh (GSA posts the next year's files around August)

1. Make a folder outside the repo's tracked files, e.g. `.perdiem-src/` (ignored by `.gitignore`), with `gsa/`, `geonames/`, `census/` inside.
2. Download the new FY rates XLSX to `gsa/rates.xlsx` and the new ZIP code file to `gsa/zips.xlsx` from the GSA per diem files page. Get GeoNames `US.zip` and unzip `US.txt` into `geonames/`. Put the six Census files above in `census/` (they only change with a new census), with the `national_` prefix taken off the three name lists: `county2020.txt`, `place_by_county2020.txt`, `cousub2020.txt` (the `tab20_…` files keep their names).
3. Check the M&IE breakdown page still shows the same tiers; update `MIE` in `scripts/build_perdiem.py` if not.
4. `python3 -I scripts/build_perdiem.py --fy 2028 --src .perdiem-src --out data/perdiem-fy2028.json --report .perdiem-src/multi.json` (it fails loudly if GSA changed columns, seasons don't run whole months, or the two GSA files disagree).
5. Change `DATA_URL` in index.html to the new file, and the `FY 2027` placeholder text in the per diem markup.
6. `python3 -I scripts/check_perdiem.py --src .perdiem-src --data data/perdiem-fy2028.json` must print ALL CHECKS PASSED (it can be run again after step 7: it then expects Defense Department rates in Alaska, Hawaii and the territories). Then `node scripts/spotcheck_perdiem.mjs index.html data/perdiem-fy2028.json .perdiem-src/api-cache` (DEMO_KEY allows 10 calls an hour; the script waits and caches, so it takes about 3 hours unattended).
7. `python3 -I scripts/build_oconus.py --data data/perdiem-fy2028.json --src src-dod --out data/perdiem-fy2028.json` with that year's DoD files (section above), then the oconus test.
8. Delete the old year's JSON once the new one is live.

## Gotchas

- **Class names:** the punch strip container is `.punch` (`#dial`); the Punch in/out key is `.pkey`. Giving the key the class `punch` painted a stray ring above the strip.
- **sw.js** must stay at the site root (scope `/`). Playwright with a fake clock (`page.clock.install`) freezes the 250 ms save debounce: call `clock.runFor(400)` before reading `localStorage` in tests.
- **Colours:** keep every colour a token. The ink lives only in the INK block at the top of the stylesheet, the warm accent only in the EMBER block under it. A new answer or live indicator takes `var(--ember)`; a frame, ring or selected key takes `var(--stamp)` / `var(--stamp-wash)` (or `currentColor` in SVG). The favicon data URI, the icon PNGs and og.png bake the ember hand in (#F08A2A on graphite); regenerate them if the ember changes. The script that drew them is not in the repo (see Open decisions).
- The `pd-core` lookup block and `data/` were not touched by the reskin (byte-identical to the per diem merge).

- **Per diem:** GSA's ZIP file puts a whole ZIP in one area, so it sometimes disagrees with GSA's own county rule. Examples: East Falmouth's ZIP 02536 is rated Hyannis (GSA's file), and Edwards AFB's ZIPs are rated Bakersfield, not Los Angeles. The ZIP answer follows GSA's file, because that's what GSA's own site and API return.
- **GSA API DEMO_KEY** allows only 10 calls, then can lock out for hours (a 429 with retry-after of about 4.5 hours on 2026-10-16), and retrying doesn't help. For the yearly spot check, get a free personal key at api.data.gov/signup (pass it as the 4th argument, never commit it), or read the rates off gsa.gov's per diem lookup page.
- The page's lookup lives between `/* pd-core:start` and `/* pd-core:end */` and must stay DOM-free, because the check scripts pull that block out and run it in Node.
- The search key (`key()` in both the Python build and the page) spells out St/Ste/Ft/Mt. State words are matched before that, or "MT" would become "mount".
- **Sliding thumbs:** every `.seg` except AM/PM gets class `slide`, and its thumb (`.seg.slide::before`) is placed through `--px`, `--pw` and `--po`, set by `placePill()` in the hours script (startup, resize, fonts ready, each `build()`) and by `pill()` in the per diem script after a view switch (a hidden view has no widths to measure). Both are needed. `#breakCount` hides its thumb and styles its keys by `aria-pressed`.
- The per diem CSS is all scoped to `#pdView`, `.viewbar` or `body[data-view="pd"]`. Keep it that way, because the hours view shares class names like `.stat`, `.k`, `.big` and `.label`.
- After FY 2027 ends (Sep 30, 2027) the view still works but shows a red "FY 2027 rates; newer ones may apply" badge until the data is refreshed.

- **Layout markup:** `<form id="form">` is the `.grid` wrapper holding `.col-in` (entry card + options card) and the result section. On phones `.col-in` is `display: contents` and the cards are reordered with `order`. `#dec` stays as a hidden span because the decimal copier reads it; `#mins` and `#brk` live in `.hmline`. The stylesheet is one sheet in this order: INK block, EMBER block, surface tokens, base, components, per diem (scoped to `#pdView`), months (scoped to `#moView`), then the ≤640px phone rules, ≤400/360px narrow-phone rules and the ≥900px desktop rules. The tiny reset `<style>` near the top of `<head>` stays separate.

- **The decimal change alters old behaviour.** Before, `9.30` meant 9:30. It now means 9:18. The iOS decimal keypad has no colon, so phone users who typed `9.30` for 9:30 now need `930`. The badge and the hint text explain this.
- Saved state from before decimal input has no `week` key. On first load, `normalise()` rewrites its dotted times to colon form (`9.30` becomes `9:30`), so old saves keep their meaning. The rewrite runs only once, because the next save adds `week`.
- `normalise()` rebuilds state from a fixed shape. New stored fields must be added to `DEFAULT` and `normalise()`, or they are wiped on reload.
- If `build()` throws at startup, the saved state is copied to `shift-hours-v1-backup`, the page starts from defaults, and the error is logged. Keep new code defensive.
- `adopt()` replaces `S` with another tab's state and rebuilds. Anything holding a reference to an old `S` object (like the `moved` map of format-switch notes) must be reset there.
- `computeDay()` returns `state`: `empty`, `ok`, `open` (something missing) or `bad`. `renderWeek()` relies on it to decide which days are "not counted".
- The 12/24-hour toggle converts every stored time, including the week rows. Any new time field must be added to that list (with a label, used in the note shown when an invalid entry becomes valid in the other format).
- Test hooks: `window.__hours` (`parse`, `toMin`, `roundMin`, `span`, `compute`, `computeDay`, `renderWeek`, `normalise`, `reapply`, `tickLive`, `flush`, `S`), `window.__months` (`holidays`, `month`, `show`, `go`), `window.__perdiem` (`core`, `compute`, `setView`, `ensureData`, `S`), `window.__backup` (`bundle`, `encode`, `decode`, `describe`). `flush` and `reapply` are also used by the backup script, so they are not test-only.
- Tests: `tests/run.sh` (needs node, Playwright's Chromium and python3 for the static server; no network needed: online the page loads its Google fonts, offline it falls back to system fonts). Test names: `smoke backup punch oconus audit review months sweep`. The full run is about 4.5 minutes, about 3 of them `audit` (its four sizes run side by side). `audit` reads the pre-fix page from git history (`git show 2155b07:index.html`), so it needs a clone deep enough to hold that commit. With Playwright's fake clock, build dates in the browser's timezone (the audit test sets `process.env.TZ` to match `timezoneId`), or night shifts land on the wrong day.
- **Clock changes:** only punched shifts are corrected (`clockChange()`, `week[i].adj`). If you add a new way to set the start time, decide whether it is a new shift (clear `liveAt`/`outAt` as `handEdit()` does) or still the punch.
- The repo is public, so keep this file about the site only.
